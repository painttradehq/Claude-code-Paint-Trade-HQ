## Copyright in AI-written code — Australian position checked (7 Oct)
- Josh asked whether copyright protection differs for an app built entirely by AI. Checked (web): Australia has **no copyright register** for anyone, human or AI, so nothing to "register" either way. The difference is in whether copyright **exists**: the Copyright Act 1968 needs a human author with independent intellectual effort (IceTV v Nine 2009 HCA; Telstra v Phone Directories 2010 FCAFC; Acohs v Ucorp 2012 FCAFC — computer-produced material with no sufficiently involved human author had no copyright). Australia has no UK-style "computer-generated works" clause. Anthropic's commercial terms and Emergent's terms assign any rights in the output to the customer, but they cannot create a copyright the Act does not grant. The CAIRG reform work (draft legislation flagged for Q1 2027) is about training/licensing, not authorship. Practical protection where copyright is doubtful: trade secret/confidentiality, contract (Terms, assignments), and records of Josh's direction and review.
