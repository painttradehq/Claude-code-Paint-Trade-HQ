# C9 — Sign by drawing, and the quote copy-protected (spec v1.1, 28 Nov 2026)

Prototype `c1-crew-shell.html` (248,853 bytes, md5 e41450cdff7c2b85386a9b1542f24e36) at 390 × 844, then 360 × 800. ## 1. What this round builds

**A. Sign by drawing** (`?screen=doc&id=d1` → Sign this document). The sign sheet opens on **Draw**: a pad like the one a client signs a quote with — a white box, "Sign here with your finger", **Clear** — plus a baseline (the client's has none, and its hint ends "or mouse"). **Type** is the other tab and is today's sheet unchanged (the name field and the script preview). Confirm & sign sends the drawing as a PNG. An empty pad gives one line. Nothing else on the document screen changes.

**B. The drawing prints on the signed copy** (`?screen=contract-pdf&id=d1&sig=drawn`, a prototype-only preview of the PDF's signature block; `&sig=typed` for today's look). On the contract PDF the crew member's drawn signature sits above their name, the way the owner's uploaded signature sits above the countersign name today. A typed signature prints the name alone, as today. The PDF is made when the owner countersigns, so the image appears on documents countersigned after this round; stored copies are not regenerated. One intended exception: a stored copy that can no longer be read is rendered again on request (as today), and that render now carries the drawing.

**C. The quote is copy-protected** (`?screen=quote&id=j1`). On the crew Quote screen nothing on the paper can be selected; copy, cut, the long-press menu and dragging are refused; printing the page shows none of the paper. The job page (addresses, paint names, the scope) stays selectable: the protection is the quote's alone. This is a deterrent, not a lock: a photo of the screen is still possible, and the words still travel to the phone to be shown.

**D. C8's open test line** (§5.4).
