Every tracking app for tradies makes the same promise to the owner and the same threat to the crew: we know where you are. We didn't want that in Paint Trade HQ, so the time clock works differently.

**Two moments, nothing else.** When a crew member signs in on site, the app records the time, the address and the distance from the job. When they sign out, it does the same again. Between those two taps the phone is not tracked. The screen says so in plain words under the Sign out button, because the crew should know it too.

**What the owner gets from those two moments** is more than enough to run the week:

- A timesheet that writes itself, with regular and overtime hours split by your own thresholds (eight hours a day and 38 a week by default).
- Labour cost flowing straight into the live job, so margin is a number you can see before the invoice goes out, not after.
- A flag if someone signs in outside the job's radius. Not an alarm, a flag, for you to read in context.

**What the crew get** is a phone app that asks very little of them: today's job with the address and directions, a big Sign in button, a daily report with photos at the end of the day, and nothing about prices or margins. They can't see what the client is paying, and that is deliberate.

**Operating hours** do the reminding. Set your start and end time and working days in Settings, and the app nudges people to sign in before the start and out at the end. An auto sign-out time closes the session someone forgot about at knock-off, so the Friday timesheet isn't a detective job.

If you want a painter's take on it: the clock is there to make payroll honest in both directions. Hours get paid, and hours get costed into the job. That needs two taps a day, not a dot on a map all afternoon.
