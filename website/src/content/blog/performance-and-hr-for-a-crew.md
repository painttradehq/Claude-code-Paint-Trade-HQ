HR software is built for companies with an HR department. A painting business has an owner who is also the foreman, and a crew of three to fifteen who need to be hired properly, told when they have done well, told when something needs fixing, and given the right gear. That is the whole brief. Paint Trade HQ's HR side does those things and stops there.

## Performance: say it, keep it, show the trend

**Validations** are the good calls: the clean cut-in, the client who rang to say thanks, the early start on the wet day. **Coaching notes** are the quiet corrections. Each person has both, with a **score trend over time**, so a review is a conversation about a record rather than about whoever had the loudest week.

Each crew member sees their own validations and coaching notes in the crew app, and only their own.

## Contracts: your wording, their signature, on the phone

Hiring properly means an agreement both of you have signed. In the app you keep **your own employment-agreement templates**. The app points you to the business.gov.au Employment Contract Tool for wording and never supplies legal wording of its own; that is a deliberate line. A wizard fills in the fields for the person and the role, the crew member reads and signs it on their phone, you countersign, and the signed PDF is emailed to both of you and saved under "Employee contracts". Nothing signed is ever edited or deleted. The crew member can download their signed copy from the crew app any time.

## Equipment: who has the sprayer

The **Equipment** register lists the business's tools and gear and who has what. The crew can **request** equipment from their phone, and the request lands with you. It is a small thing that ends a large number of Monday-morning phone calls.

## Training, daily reports and the notice board

**Training videos** are your own videos, grouped for the crew, and the app keeps track of who has watched what, so the induction does not depend on memory. **Daily reports** are the crew's end-of-day write-up from the phone: what was done, photos, issues, materials used; you read them in a feed and reply in a thread, and the client's completion report draws on them. **Team events** is a notice board for the crew: the barbecue, the toolbox talk, the day the yard is shut.

<div class="scenario"><span class="eyebrow">A realistic fortnight</span>

Say you take on a painter with eight years' experience. Before she starts you generate her agreement from your full-time template, she signs it on her phone from the car on Sunday night, you countersign at the kitchen table, and both of you have the PDF before Monday.

Week one, the daily reports from her job are the best you have read: photos of every room, a note about a damp patch behind the laundry door you would otherwise have found out about from the client. You add a validation. Week two, she signs out twice at the right time but forgets the end-of-day photos; you add a coaching note, short and specific, and she can see it in her app before you have the conversation. Thursday she requests the second airless sprayer for Friday from the crew app; it is on the register, the apprentice has it, and you sort it with a phone call.

By the end of the month her score trend is a line going the right way, and when she asks about a rate rise in six months, you both have something better than a feeling to talk about.</div>

## Why this matters

Good people stay where they are told when they are doing well, corrected plainly when they are not, hired properly, and given the gear to do the job. None of that needs an HR department. It needs the record to be easy enough to keep that a busy owner actually keeps it.
