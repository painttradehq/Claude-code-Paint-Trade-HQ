Ask a painter where the record of a job is and you get a list: the quote is in email, the colours are in a text message, the hours are in someone's head or a notebook on the dash, the photos are on three phones, and the extra work the client asked for on day four is nowhere at all. That works until the day it has to be found.

In Paint Trade HQ the record is the **project page**, and it starts itself.

## A job appears the moment the client signs

When a client accepts a quote on their phone, the app creates the deposit invoice and a **project**. The accepted quote, the client, the address and the product colours chosen on the quote come across with it.

The **Projects page** shows every job in flight: its status, the crew assigned, and money so far. On Small business and up it also shows business performance figures across your live jobs.

## What the project page holds

Open a job and everything that matters to it is on one page or in the drawer beside it:

- **The accepted quote**, exactly as signed, with who accepted it, when, and from which address.
- **Crew assignment.** Who is on this job. The crew see it in their app as their day's work with the address.
- **Products attached**, including the colours picked on the quote. The colour question gets answered from the page, not from a text thread.
- **A before-and-after photo portfolio.** Photos from the crew's daily reports (Enterprise) and from the job itself, kept with the job. The site-visit photos stay on the quote, linked from the job.
- **Labour cost from the time clock.** The crew sign in and out on their phones; the hours land here, costed with each person's pay rates, alongside the margin, so you can see what the job cost against what you quoted.
- **Margin and close-out numbers.** What the job made, once it is done.
- **The variations.** Every priced change, with the client's typed-name approval.
- **An AI summary of where the job is** (Small business and up): Lucy reads the job and tells you in a paragraph.
- **The completion report**, sent to the client on a link, with photos.

Two kinds of notes live in two places on purpose. **Crew notes** on the job are for the crew and show in their app. **Private admin notes** live on the quote, where the crew never see them.

## Variations without the argument

Day four, and the client would like the laundry done while you are there. In the app that is a **variation**: a priced change on the job, sent on a link, which the client approves with a typed name. It sits on the project page next to the original quote, priced, approved and dated, and it is on the record when you raise the final invoice. Nobody has to remember a conversation in a doorway.

## The completion report

When the job is done, the **completion report** goes to the client on a link: what was done, room by room, the crew, and the photos. On Enterprise it draws on the crew's daily reports, so the client sees the job the way it actually went. It is the last thing the client gets from you, and the one worth forwarding.

<div class="scenario"><span class="eyebrow">A realistic eight months later</span>

Say a client rings in June about a job you finished in October. There is a mark on the hallway wall and they are sure it was not there before. With the record in five places, that call is a bad afternoon.

With the project page it is two minutes. Open the job. The before photos from the quote show the hallway on day one. The completion report shows it on the last day, the photo the client received at the time. The colour and product are on the page, so the touch-up tin is the right one. The hours show two people for a day and a half, which is what you quoted. Whether you touch it up as goodwill or not is your call, and now it is a call you make with the facts in front of you.

The same page answers the quieter questions too: what the job cost in hours against what you quoted, and what the margin actually was once the variation and the extra day went in.</div>

## Why this matters for a painting business

A painting business runs on small jobs, and small jobs leave small records that get lost. The project page makes the record a by-product of doing the work: the quote creates the job, the signature creates the invoice, the time clock creates the hours, the crew's photos create the portfolio, the variation creates its own paper trail. Little is typed twice, and a year on, the job is still exactly where you left it.

<p class="plan-note">The Projects page, project details, variations and the completion report are on every plan. The AI job summary and the business performance figures on the Projects page are on Small business and up; daily reports from the crew are on Enterprise.</p>
