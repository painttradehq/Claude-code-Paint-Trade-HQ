There is a lot of noise about AI in trade software, so let us be plain about what Lucy is. She is an assistant that lives inside your Paint Trade HQ workspace, reads your own data, and does a short list of useful things with it. She is not a chatbot bolted on the side, and she is not magic.

## What she knows

Lucy knows **which page you are on**. Open a quote and she can say "You're looking at EST-0042" and answer about it. She answers from **your workspace's data only**: your quotes, jobs, clients, invoices, crew hours. She never sees another business's data, and the crew never see her at all; there are no AI features in the crew app.

Notice the last line. She shows where the answer came from, so you can check it.

## What she does

- **The morning brief.** On the dashboard each morning: what is on today, who to chase, written from your own quotes, jobs and invoices.
- **Drafts.** Client messages and quote text: the cover message on a quote, a follow-up to someone who has gone quiet, a tidier version of what you wrote.
- **Summaries.** A client's history in a paragraph before you ring them. Where a job is up to, on the project page.
- **Price suggestions** from your Library when you are quoting. Starting points for you to check, never numbers that go out on their own.
- **Reminders and ideas** you dictate. Tell her, and she keeps them.

## What she does not do

- She does not send anything. **Everything she writes is a draft for you to check.** You read it, change it if you like, and you press send.
- She does not estimate from photos or plans, and she does not check the crew's photos. Pricing comes from your Library and your measurements.
- She does not see the crew, and the crew do not see her.
- She does not learn from your data for anyone else. Lucy runs on Anthropic's Claude, and under Anthropic's commercial terms your data is not used to train models.

Every AI call for your business is listed in the **AI audit log**, so you can see what was asked and when.

<div class="scenario"><span class="eyebrow">A realistic day</span>

6:40 am, coffee, laptop. The brief says two quotes have been out for more than a week, one invoice is five days overdue, and the Harbour Street job finishes today. You ask Lucy to draft a follow-up for the older quote. It comes back friendly and short, in your voice; you change one word and send it from the client's page.

9:15, on site, the client asks what the quote came to and whether you sent the second one. You open the quote on the tablet and ask Lucy. She tells you the total, that the second one is still a draft, and lists her sources. You send it from the ute.

4:30, a new enquiry rings while you are driving. Before you call back you open the client, read Lucy's summary of the two jobs you did for them in 2024 and the colour they chose, and the call starts well.

None of that is clever. It is the admin you would have done anyway, done in a minute instead of twenty, with the facts pulled from your own records instead of your memory.</div>

## Why it matters for a painting business

The owner of a painting business is also the estimator, the account manager and the debt collector, usually after six o'clock. Lucy does not take those jobs away. She shortens them, by having the records in front of her and a draft ready, so the follow-up gets sent and the overdue invoice gets chased on the day rather than the week after.

<p class="plan-note">Lucy is on Small business and Enterprise. She is admin-only; the crew app has no AI features. Her drafts and summaries are starting points for you to check.</p>
