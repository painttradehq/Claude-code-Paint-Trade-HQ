Most quoting software starts with the quote. Paint Trade HQ starts one step earlier, with a Library, and that choice is the reason a quote takes minutes instead of an evening.

**Items, tasks and products.** The Library holds the things you actually sell: ceilings, walls, doors, skirting, fences, each with a rate per unit, the number of coats, and the labour minutes it takes. Tasks cover the preparation and the odd jobs. Products hold the paint and sundries with a supplier price and your price. Items carry a photo, so the crew and the client both know what "two-sided door, semi-gloss" looks like.

**Then the quote is assembly, not arithmetic.** Start from the client and the address, add areas (interior rooms from a picker, or an exterior as four faces), and pull lines from the Library by category. Measurements multiply through the rates. Repairs sit as sub-rows. Optional lines are things the client can tick on themselves when they read the quote on their phone. If you type something the Library doesn't have, you can add it to the Library straight from the quote, so the next job is faster still.

**Rates are ex-GST; quotes can show prices including GST.** The maths is the app's job. Your job is to keep the Library honest, which is a half-hour once and a minute whenever a price changes.

**What this changes on site.** Because the quote was built from items with minutes attached, the live job already knows roughly how long it should take, and the time clock tells you how long it did. That gap, job by job, is the most useful number a painting business can watch, and it falls out of the Library for free.

A practical note for anyone moving across: bringing your existing price list in is part of setting up. It is included on Enterprise and quoted on the other plans, and either way you only do it once.
