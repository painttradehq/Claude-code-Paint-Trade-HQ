People ask whether clients can pay by card inside Paint Trade HQ. They can't, by design, and it's worth saying why before saying what happens instead.

**Card payments cost the painter.** A processing fee on a four-thousand-dollar repaint is real money, and most residential clients are perfectly happy to pay a tradesperson by bank transfer. So the app never holds card details, never charges your client, and never sits between you and your money. You get paid the way you already get paid; the app makes the paperwork around it tidy.

**Invoices follow the quote.** When a client accepts a quote, the deposit invoice is created for you from the payment schedule on that quote (thirty per cent deposit, forty progress, thirty final by default, or whatever you set). Progress and final invoices follow the same schedule. Each invoice has a public page the client can read on their phone and a PDF with your bank details on it.

**Record the payment, and the balance updates.** When the money lands, record it against the invoice: bank transfer, cash, card at your own terminal, cheque, whichever it was. The invoice shows paid or part-paid, the job's money-so-far moves, and the client's page in the CRM shows it too. Credit notes are there for when you need to adjust, and payment reminders can go out automatically on the schedule you choose.

**GST is handled.** Your rates are ex-GST, invoices show GST, and the client's page shows the total they owe including it. Our own subscription is priced the same way: ex-GST, with the GST figure shown next to it.

**One more thing the client sees:** a review request after the job, with your Google review link, by email or SMS. Getting paid and getting the next job are, in the end, the same workflow.
