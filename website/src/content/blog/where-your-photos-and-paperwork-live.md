Photos are the memory of a painting business, and most of them are lost within a year. They are on the apprentice's old phone, in a chat thread with the client, or in a camera roll of eleven thousand pictures with no way to find the one of the cracked cornice from the Preston job. The paperwork is not much better: the signed quote is an email attachment, the invoice PDF is in the accounting program, the employment agreement is in a drawer.

Paint Trade HQ puts all of it with the job or the person it belongs to, and lets you decide whose storage it sits in.

## Two places it can live

By default, everything goes to **Paint Trade HQ cloud**. Nothing to set up.

Or you connect **your own Google Drive** in Settings › Business › Files & storage. The app creates a "Paint Trade HQ" folder in your Drive and one sub-folder per job, made at the first upload for that job. Photos and PDFs land there as they are made. The point of this option is simple: **the files are yours, in your Drive, and you keep them if you ever leave.**

Microsoft OneDrive appears as an option on the same page but is not set up on the server yet, and the page says so.

## What goes there, and from where

Files arrive from the work, not from a filing session:

- **Quote photos**, taken on the site visit and marked up with arrows, circles and text, stay with the quote and the job.
- **Daily report photos** (Enterprise): the crew's photos from the phone each day land with that job.
- **The before-and-after portfolio** on the project page is built from those.
- **PDFs**: every quote, invoice, credit note and completion report has one.
- **Signed documents**: the client's accepted quote with who signed it, when and from which address; signed variations; and on Enterprise, employment agreements signed on the phone and countersigned by you, emailed to both and saved under "Employee contracts". Nothing signed is ever edited or deleted.

## Who can see what

Each business has its own workspace, separate from every other business, and that separation is tested automatically on every release. Inside your business, the crew see their own job's pages in the crew app and nothing of the admin side. Team admins see the pages you give them. Clients see only the links you send: the quote, the variation, the invoice, the completion report. Each is a long random link and needs no account.

## Getting it out, and leaving

You own your data. Quotes, invoices, credit notes, reports and signed documents export as PDF; daily reports, timesheets and sign-in records export as CSV; and you can ask for a full export. If you close the account, the data is kept for 30 days in case you change your mind, then deleted from live systems, with backups overwritten within a further 60 days. If you used your own Google Drive, the photos and PDFs are already sitting in your folder and stay there.

<div class="scenario"><span class="eyebrow">Three realistic requests</span>

**The floor.** Six weeks after a job, a client sends a photo of a scuff on a timber floor and asks what you are going to do about it. You open the job: the site-visit photos from the quote show the same scuff, circled, with your note "existing, under the rug". You send that photo back with a kind sentence. Done.

**The insurer.** Your insurer asks for a copy of a crew member's signed employment agreement. It is under "Employee contracts" in your storage, the signed PDF that was emailed to both of you on the day, unchanged since.

**The move.** Three years on, you sell the business, or change software, or retire. The quotes, invoices and reports export as PDF, the hours and reports as CSV, and because you connected your own Drive back at the start, every job's photos are already in a folder with the street name on it.</div>

## Why this matters

Photos settle arguments, PDFs settle accounts, and signed documents settle disputes. They only do that if they can be found. Putting them with the job, in storage you control, is a small decision at set-up that pays off on the worst day of a job rather than the best.
